Why Blake, now

Five sentences for an age of machines

Blake made a mythology about measurement, making and the mind that closes on itself. He wrote it two centuries before anyone built a machine that learns. These are his words, as he engraved or wrote them. The notes beside them are ours.

  1. Reason or the ratio of all we have already known. is not the same that it shall be when we know more.There is No Natural Religion · about 1788

    A system built from what is already known can measure only that. Blake saw the limit, and saw past it.

  2. If it were not for the Poetic or Prophetic character. the Philosophic & Experimental would soon be at the ratio of all things & stand still, unable to do other than repeat the same dull round over againThere is No Natural Religion · Conclusion

    Something has to come from outside the round. He called it the Poetic character.

  3. I must Create a System, or be enslav’d by another Mans I will not Reason & Compare: my business is to CreateJerusalem · plate 10 · Los at his furnace

    Every system we think inside was made by someone. Los chooses to be a maker of them.

  4. He who would do good to another, must do it in Minute Particulars General Good is the plea of the scoundrel hypocrite & flattererJerusalem · plate 55

    Care is done to one person at a time. Claims made on behalf of everyone deserve a second look.

  5. May God us keep From Single vision & Newtons sleepLetter to Thomas Butts · 1802

    One measure gives one reading. In the same letter Blake counts four kinds of seeing.

About this site

An independent project

No words are put into Blake’s mouth here, and no pictures are made in his manner by a machine.

williamblake.ai is made by Alexander Sabine, an educator who builds simulations and films that teach frontier science through time, process and care. It is not affiliated with any Blake society, archive, museum or estate.

What will stand here is a way in: Blake’s books as rooms to walk through, each plate linked to its scholarly record, with the ideas of present-day science set beside the poems that met them first.
